The National Cybersecurity Authority has published control sets for a growing range of organizations, including private entities that are not classified as critical infrastructure <Verify />. For many companies, this is the first time a national framework applies to them directly.

## What changes

Cybersecurity moves from "good practice" to an expectation you may need to evidence. Boards, customers and government clients will increasingly ask how you meet it, especially in tenders and supply chains.

## Where to start

- **Know your scope.** Confirm which control set applies to your organization and which systems are in scope.
- **Run a gap assessment.** Compare current practice with the controls and rank gaps by risk, not by how easy they are to close.
- **Fix identity first.** Strong authentication, privileged-access management and joiner-mover-leaver processes close many gaps at once.
- **Protect the data you hold.** Classify sensitive data and control how it leaves the organization, including through screens and printouts.
- **Prepare evidence as you go.** Policies, logs and test results are what auditors will ask for.

## Common pitfalls

Buying tools before defining ownership; treating compliance as a one-off project; and overlooking third parties who have access to your systems.
