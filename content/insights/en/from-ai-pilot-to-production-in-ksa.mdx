With 2026 declared the Year of AI <Verify />, almost every Saudi organization has an AI pilot somewhere. Far fewer have AI that real customers or citizens use every day. The gap is rarely the model. It is the decisions made, or skipped, before the pilot starts.

## 1. Start from an outcome, not a use case

"Use AI in customer service" is a theme. "Resolve 30% of permit enquiries on WhatsApp without staff involvement" is an outcome. A pilot built around a measurable outcome has a clear finish line and a clear business case.

## 2. Use real data from day one

Pilots that run on sample data prove that the technology works in general, not that it works for you. Agree early which systems the pilot can read from and write to, and who approves access.

## 3. Decide where it will run in production

If production must be in-Kingdom or on-premise, the pilot should be too. Moving later means re-testing, re-approving and often re-buying. Settle data residency under the PDPL and the relevant NCA controls before you start.

## 4. Design the human checkpoints

Agentic AI acts, not just answers. Define which steps need human approval, who gives it and how every action is logged. This is what security, legal and audit teams will ask about, so answer it first.

## 5. Plan the handover

Who runs the system after go-live? Production AI needs monitoring, retraining and change control. Build knowledge transfer into the pilot so your team can own it.

## A fixed scope makes it real

A proof of value with a fixed scope, real data and agreed success measures gives leadership evidence, not opinions. Scale what works; stop what does not.
